Questions 3–5Only the stable path is an equilibrium

Fig. 1The paths we rule out
\(\dot k=0\)\(\dot c=0\)stable path (equilibrium)\(k\lt 0\) or \(c\lt 0\)
Why each path fails
  • 1a \(c\) reaches the \(k\)-axis at a finite \(T\): impossible, since \(c(T)=c(0)e^{\int_0^T (f'(k)-\delta-\rho)\,ds}\ge c(0)e^{-(\delta+\rho)T}>0\).
  • 1b \(k\) reaches the \(c\)-axis at a finite \(T\) with \(c(T)>0\): there \(\dot k=-c\lt 0\), so the path cannot continue with \(k\ge 0\). Infeasible.
  • 2 \(k\to 0\) asymptotically: left of \(k^*\) and above \(\dot k=0\), \(\dot k\lt 0\) and \(\ddot k\lt 0\), so \(k\) falls at least linearly and hits zero in finite time (case 1b).
  • 3.1 \(k\to\tilde k\lt\bar k\) with \(c\to0\): near \((\tilde k,0)\), \(\dot k\to f(\tilde k)-(n+\delta)\tilde k>0\), so \(k\) cannot stop at \(\tilde k\). It keeps rising to \(\bar k\): case 3.1 turns into 3.2.
  • 3.2 \(k\to\bar k\) with \(c\to0\): after \(t_g\), \(k>k_{gold}\) so \(r\lt n\). With \(B=-\int_0^{t_g}(r-n)\,ds\), for \(t\ge t_g\) we get \(k(t)\,e^{-\int_0^t(r-n)\,ds}\ge k_{gold}\,e^{B}>0\), so the TVC fails.

What to notice
  • \(k\) cannot move on impact; \(c\) jumps vertically onto the new stable path. Both cases reach the same new steady state.
  • \(\sigma=1\): the income effect wins and \(c\) jumps up. \(\sigma=0.2\) (high IES): the substitution effect toward the higher \(r\) wins and \(c\) jumps down.
